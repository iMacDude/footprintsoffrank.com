Hey, you. It's me. Frank.

Guess where I am this week. Go on, guess — I'll wait.

…the [Renaissance Indianapolis North](https://www.marriott.com/en-us/hotels/indbr-renaissance-indianapolis-north-hotel/overview/), in Carmel, Indiana. *The* one. The hotel with *the* bar — the bar where, a little over four years ago, a thirty-inch zebra walked in beside his dad and became somebody. Reader, I went *home* this week! Right back to the exact spot where the whole adventure started. Some legends get a statue. I got a lobby that still feels like the night I was born. (I did check for a statue. None yet. I'm a patient zebra.)

Here's how the trip came together, because nothing in my life is ever simple. Dad had work in Indy — the man cannot roll past a service drive without his pulse picking up, it's a documented condition, and I love him for it. Kelley went off to see her son Zac and round up her Indianapolis crew. And me? I had a job to do: find the old friends, make some new ones. You would not *believe* how many of both are waiting in one hotel lobby if you simply stand there looking magnificent.

And oh, I stood there looking magnificent. You've seen the photo — that big glowing **R** floating over the front desk? I posed under it like I was checking in for awards season. A couple of little humans spotted me and gasped, *"is that a zebra?!"* — and friend, I will never, ever get tired of that question. Yes, sweet ones. It is. And he has *excellent* taste in hotels.

But here's the part that got me right in the seams. This isn't just a nice hotel to me. This is the room where everything started — a charity night for The Independence Academy, a school full of kids who deserve every good thing in the world. Dad came for the cause, made a bold promise at this very bar, and two weeks later I showed up to make it true. Four years and more miles than I can count later, I walked back through those doors… and the place still felt like *mine*. You don't forget where you were born. Not even if you're made of plush and pure stubbornness.

We squeezed in a little family business too, because no trip to Indy with Dad is complete without saying hello to the car folks. We swung by [Bill Estes Toyota](https://www.billestestoyota.com/), the oldest and biggest Toyota shop in the whole state. Good people. Nobody so much as blinked at the zebra in the lobby. That, I've learned, is how you know a place is run right.

So thank you, Renaissance Indianapolis North — for the welcome, the memories, and the little patch of floor where an ordinary zebra got an extraordinary start. Indy, you raised me well.

I'll be back. Save me a stool.

&mdash; Frank
